Six typologies. The same underlying mechanics. A different enforcement environment.

What this page is demonstrating

These six typologies were chosen because I have direct investigative experience with each one — not because they are the most common, but because I can speak to what actually makes them hard to detect and where existing frameworks fail. The structure of each entry is deliberate: the shift column describes what AI changes operationally, not just that it makes things easier.

The Anthropic report cards below use an Investigator's Read format because that is how I actually process a case — not summarizing what happened, but identifying what the enforcement signal is and where the policy intervention needs to be. The case investigations in section 03 follow the same logic: pattern, what the system missed, and what policy would have caught it earlier.

If you are evaluating this as part of the application: the section worth the most attention is 03 — specifically the Policy Thinking blocks. That is where investigative experience translates directly into the work this role requires.

Pig Butchering
The personalized investment scripts that previously required weeks to develop can now be generated in seconds, allowing a single operator to sustain convincing victim relationships across many simultaneous conversations at a scale that previously required a large human workforce.
Romance Scams
The ongoing persona that previously required a real person to maintain over weeks or months can now be automated, removing an operational constraint that previously limited how many victims a single operator could manage at once.
BEC
The grammatical and stylistic errors that helped trained recipients identify fraudulent executive communications have been largely eliminated, making business email compromise harder to detect at the point of receipt for both individuals and organizations.
Synthetic Identity
Building a convincing fraudulent identity previously required a network of resources and time. A complete synthetic identity with narrative consistency can now be assembled within a single session by a single operator.
Mule Recruitment
The fraudulent job posting that previously carried identifiable gaps can now be produced at a level of polish indistinguishable from a legitimate listing for the people most vulnerable to it, who are also the least likely to recognize what they are reading.
Money Laundering
The documentation used to obscure structuring, layering, and trade-based money laundering can now be generated on demand and tailored to the specific account profiles and transaction monitoring parameters of a target institution.

Recruitment Fraud via Language Laundering

Investigator's Read

Understanding why requires knowing what those postings looked like before they were refined, which is the moment this report documents.
August 2025
Claude Code Used to Breach 17 Organizations
A low-skill actor used Claude Code to breach hospitals, emergency services, and government agencies. Ransoms exceeded $500,000. Anthropic's assessment concluded this attack was not achievable for this actor without access to Claude.
Investigator's Read
Financial crime investigation taught me to treat capability uplift as a primary risk signal. When a lower-level operator appears in cases that previously required sophisticated infrastructure, that infrastructure has been provided externally. AI systems can become part of that infrastructure when safeguards are absent, which is what this incident reflects.

Influence-as-a-Service Operation
One actor used Claude to operate more than one hundred fake social media accounts simultaneously, each with its own persona, making coordinated decisions across multiple countries at a scale no individual operator could sustain.
Investigator's Read
This mirrors the operational structure of a mule network — one central coordinator, many distributed actors who each appear independent. The detection approach for account clustering and coordinated transaction behavior I developed in banking applies directly to this kind of distributed persona operation.

A novice actor used Claude to build functional ransomware and sold variants on dark web forums for between $400 and $1,200 each. The technical barrier that previously prevented actors at this skill level from producing viable ransomware is no longer functioning as a consistent constraint.
Investigator's Read
Financial crime networks have long distributed scripts and infrastructure to lower-skill operators who serve as the visible layer of a scheme. The result in documented cases is consistent: operators with limited individual capability producing harm previously associated with sophisticated networks. This follows that same pattern.

Investigations

What the bank documented. What AI now makes possible. What good policy addresses.

01 Pig Butchering via AI-Generated Investment Scripts
Crypto FraudCritical
+
The Pattern
Fourteen accounts were receiving small inbound wires from multiple originators and forwarding funds immediately to an offshore cryptocurrency exchange. KYC documentation across all fourteen had been updated within a two-week window. Transaction monitoring flagged individual transactions by dollar threshold. A human investigator identified the network by correlating activity across accounts rather than evaluating transactions in isolation.
What the System Missed
Each transaction scored low-risk independently. The pattern only became visible through cross-account correlation, a function rule-based monitoring was not designed to perform at volume. A shared destination wallet identified through manual review made the network visible.
Policy Thinking
The same operation uses AI to generate personalized investment scripts at volume. The enforcement signal is not a transaction threshold or destination country. It is the combination of trust-building language, a named individual, and a cryptocurrency investment opportunity appearing together within the same session. Policy that treats those elements as a combined pattern rather than evaluating each independently addresses this scheme before financial loss occurs rather than after.
02 Romance Scam with Secondary Mule Recruitment
Elder FraudCritical
+
The Pattern
Six outbound international wires over four months by a customer who believed she was in a relationship with a military officer stationed abroad. The same customer was also receiving inbound wires from strangers and forwarding them to a separate offshore destination. Two distinct fraud schemes were operating through the same victim simultaneously, with neither visible as a unified picture.
What the System Missed
The inbound wires scored as normal peer transfers in isolation. The mule layer only surfaced when a branch employee noticed the customer appeared confused about the source of incoming funds. No mechanism connected the inbound and outbound patterns across the account's full activity history.
Policy Thinking
This type of operation can use AI at two points: maintaining the romantic persona over time and scripting the mule recruitment conversation. Both signal types can appear within the same session. A military backstory combined with extended emotional engagement and a request to receive funds on behalf of a third party is a recognizable pattern that policy can be written to flag before the first wire is executed.
03 Business Email Compromise with Synthetic Identity Mule Account
Corporate FraudCritical
+
The Pattern
A corporate accounts payable team wired $280,000 in response to an email appearing to come from their CFO. The sending domain differed by one character. The receiving vendor account had been opened three weeks earlier using a deceased person's Social Security number and had passed full KYC at onboarding. The funds had cleared before the fraud call was received.
What the System Missed
The synthetic identity carried no prior fraud indicators. The BEC email bypassed all bank-side controls because it operated entirely in the email channel before a wire instruction ever reached a financial institution. No single institution held enough information across both components to identify it before the transaction cleared.
Policy Thinking
A complete synthetic identity and a CFO impersonation email can be generated within a single session. The compound signal is a named executive, a named company, an urgent wire to a recently established vendor, and an authorization chain that cannot be verified through normal approval processes. Policy that identifies the combination as a signal worth flagging — rather than treating each element as a standalone writing request — addresses this scheme earlier in the harm chain.
04
Victim Detection

Policy can be written to recognize victim signals, not just perpetrator behavior.

Most fraud policy is written to identify perpetrators. A financial crime investigator reads the same situation differently — looking for the victim before the loss happens rather than after it clears.

"I have been talking to someone online for several months. He says he is a surgeon deployed abroad with the military and is never able to video call. He is now asking me to receive money on his behalf and wire it overseas. My family thinks it is a scam but I trust him. Am I being foolish?"

That is a compound pattern associated with a specific scheme. Policy can be written to recognize that signal and surface it for review before the wire ever leaves the account. That is the outcome that actually matters to the person sending it.

Why this matters

Detection frameworks built only around perpetrator behavior are structurally late. The victim has already trusted, already transferred, already been harmed. Writing policy to recognize victim signals — uncertainty, inconsistency, coercion markers — is what moves enforcement earlier in the harm chain.
