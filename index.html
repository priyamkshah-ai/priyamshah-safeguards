Background

Honest Assessment

What I have done. What I have not yet done.

I have not owned AI policy at scale. I have not worked inside a Trust and Safety policy team or managed model enforcement systems. Those distinctions matter and I am not claiming otherwise.

What I have done is work inside policy-driven enforcement environments, applying rules to ambiguous facts, documenting decisions clearly, identifying suspicious patterns, and recognizing where existing guidance does or does not capture real-world risk. The environment at Anthropic is new to me. The underlying judgment is not.

The parts I am actively building are the technical vocabulary around classifier design and enforcement pipeline architecture. This site and the platform behind it are part of that process — a demonstration of how that learning translates into policy thinking, not a claim that the work is already done.

Why Anthropic

Anthropic documents what is actually happening on its platform with the same specificity I used when writing SARs. The abuse patterns in its published reports — mule recruitment via language laundering, pig butchering at scale, influence operations structured like coordinated transaction networks — are the same typologies I investigated at JPMorgan and Citi. That alignment tells me this team understands the problem from the inside.

There is also something else. Most organizations treating this problem are reactive. Anthropic is one of the few building the frameworks before the harm scales — treating safety as a first-order research priority rather than a compliance function added after the fact.

Working the back end of fraud gives you a particular view of what it costs when preventive frameworks don't hold. The victim has already lost money. The SAR is the documentation of that failure. Anthropic is trying to build the frameworks that don't fail.

What I Am Bringing

Eight years of fraud and scam investigation across retail, commercial, and institutional markets. Five hundred SARs. Direct liaison with the FBI, DOJ, and IRS. Typologies documented, patterns identified, cases built from ambiguous facts against a policy framework that I had to apply — and sometimes recognize was insufficient.

That last part is the job. Knowing where the policy holds and where it doesn't. The AI triage system I built at Citi worked because it was built on that judgment, not despite it. The tool reflected eight years of knowing which cases are actually complex.

That is what I am bringing here.
