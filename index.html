Background

I am a financial crime investigator across JPMorgan Chase and Citi, working fraud, scams, money laundering, market manipulation, brokerage AML, and sanctions evasion from across retail banking, commercial banking, and institutional markets. I have filed more than 500 Suspicious Activity Reports, processed subpoenas from the FBI, DOJ, and IRS, and investigated many of the same fraud, scam, money movement, and victim-exploitation patterns that now appear in AI-enabled abuse reports and published misuse data.

I also applied AI to the investigation workflow, designing a structured prompt that scored and ranked cases by complexity before any investigator opened a file. The prompt worked because it was built on investigative logic, not just automation.

Honest Assessment

What I have done. What I have not yet done.

I have not owned AI policy at scale. I have not worked inside a Trust and Safety policy team or managed model enforcement systems. Those distinctions matter and I am not claiming otherwise.

What I have done is work inside policy-driven enforcement environments, applying rules to ambiguous facts, documenting decisions clearly, identifying suspicious patterns, and recognizing where existing guidance does or does not capture real-world risk. The environment at Anthropic is new to me. The underlying judgment is not.

The parts I am actively building are the technical vocabulary around classifier design and enforcement pipeline architecture. This site and the platform behind it are part of that process — a demonstration of how that learning translates into policy thinking, not a claim that the work is already done.

Why Anthropic

Anthropic documents what is actually happening on its platform with the same specificity I used when writing SARs. The abuse patterns in its published reports are the same typologies I investigated at JPMorgan and Citi. That alignment tells me this team understands the problem from the inside. Investigative experience will matter here.

What I Am Bringing

I am not looking to leave that work behind. I am looking to move it upstream — from documenting harm after it reaches people, to helping write the policies that stop it before it does.
