Background

I am a financial crime investigator with eight years across JPMorgan Chase and Citi, working fraud, scams, money laundering, market manipulation, brokerage AML, and sanctions evasion from across retail banking, commercial banking, and institutional markets. I have filed more than 500 Suspicious Activity Reports, processed subpoenas from the FBI, DOJ, and IRS, and investigated every major scheme that now appears in Anthropic's published misuse reports.

At Citi, I identified a manual triage problem that was consuming investigation time before any actual investigation began and built an automated solution using a structured AI prompt that scored and ranked every case by complexity, so investigators always knew which cases to work first. Spotting an operational gap, applying AI to close it, and documenting it so the whole team could use it is the same thinking this role requires at a different scale.

I entered this field because the people harmed by financial crime deserve better. Most of them never see it coming, and most are never made whole. AI has made that problem significantly harder to address through investigation alone. The response that changes outcomes is better policy, built earlier in the harm chain.

Honest Assessment

What I have done. What I have not yet done.

I have not formally authored enterprise-wide AI policy frameworks and I have not held a Trust and Safety title at a technology company. Those distinctions are real.

While I have not owned AI policy at scale, I have spent my career working inside policy-driven enforcement environments, applying rules to ambiguous facts, documenting decisions clearly, and identifying where existing guidance does not capture real-world risk. That is the discipline this role requires. The environment at Anthropic is new to me. The underlying work is not.

The parts I am actively building are the technical vocabulary around classifier design and enforcement pipeline architecture. This site is part of that process — a demonstration of that learning rather than a claim made on a resume.

Why Anthropic

Anthropic publishes what is actually happening on its platform with a transparency uncommon at this scale. The schemes in its misuse reports are documented with the same specificity that financial crime investigators use when writing SARs. That tells me investigative experience will be treated as relevant expertise rather than as something adjacent to the real work.

That is the kind of environment where the work I want to do is actually possible.

What I Am Bringing

I have filed the SARs, worked the victims, traced the money, and documented the patterns across every major scheme in Anthropic's published reports. The most important thing those eight years produced is a clear understanding that the point of intervention determines the outcome. Catching harm at the point of generation rather than at the point of loss changes what is possible for real people. That is the work this role makes possible.
