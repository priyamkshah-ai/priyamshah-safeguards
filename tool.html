What This Is Demonstrating
This prototype was built to show how financial crime investigative judgment translates into fraud policy work — not to demonstrate that I can use an API. The four pre-built scenarios are real typologies from actual cases. The policy language structure (bright line prohibitions, contextual rules, classifier signals as distinct layers) reflects how enforcement decisions actually get made in practice, not a generic template.
What to look for
1.Scenario Review — The TTP breakdown reflects how I would read a case. The classifier signals identify what policy needs to catch, not just what happened.
2.Policy Drafts — The structured format separates bright line prohibitions from contextual rules deliberately. Edge cases and false positive guidance are included because over-enforcement is a real cost.
3.Gap Review — Identifies where current policy language may not capture the pattern. This is the part of the job I find most interesting.
4.Case Log — Saves decisions with analyst notes. In a real workflow, this is how you build the feedback loop between enforcement and policy revision.

Typology Policy Drafts
The output format — bright line prohibition, contextual rule, classifier signals, edge cases, false positive guidance — reflects how I think enforcement policy should be structured. Bright lines and contextual rules are distinct because conflating them produces both over- and under-enforcement. Edge cases and false positive guidance are included because they're what separate a usable policy from one that breaks in production.
